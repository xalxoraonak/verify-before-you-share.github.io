Verify Before You Share

One pause can stop a rumour. One check can protect your friends, family, and community from misinformation.

Why checking matters

Every day, posts travel faster than facts. A screenshot, a dramatic headline, or an edited video can look real even when it is not. Before forwarding something, ask yourself: Who made this? Where did it come from? Can I find it from a trusted source?

Misinformation spreads because it often plays on fear, anger, or excitement. But you do not need to be an expert to slow it down. You only need a simple habit: pause before you share.

Think first. Share wisely. Your share can either spread confusion or spread clarity.

Use the SIFT method

SIFT is an easy four-step way to check information before believing or sharing it.

Stop

Pause before reacting or forwarding.

Investigate

Check who created the content.

Find

Look for better, trusted coverage.

Trace

Go back to the original source.

Photo by ThisisEngineering on Unsplash.

Quick checks you can do today

Read beyond the headline. Reverse-search an unusual image. Check whether reliable newsrooms, official agencies, or subject experts are reporting the same information. If a post makes you feel urgent panic, treat that feeling as a reminder to verify first.

Being careful online is not about trusting nothing. It is about choosing what deserves your trust.

Be the person who checks

Share this blog, start a conversation in your class or group chat, and remind others that a few seconds of checking can prevent a lot of harm.
